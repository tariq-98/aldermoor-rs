# Aldermoor: the agreed game design

Settled with Tariq in the design interview with Codex on 23 October 2026: 54 questions, every
recommendation accepted, plus the no-spiders rule. The direction is **Breath of the Wild-inspired
visuals, RuneScape-like freedom and progression, and a modular character system where equipment
fits and behaves correctly.** Names, numbers, recipes, quests and item counts stay provisional
until a coherent draft is reviewed.

## Characters

- **Races:** humans, elves, dwarves and foxfolk. Every race can take every specialty; racial perks
  are small and never dictate a playstyle.
- **Foxfolk:** upright bodies with fully furred, expressive fox faces and short muzzles, ears and
  tails, and fur patterns. Helmets, clothing and capes are adapted to clear their ears and tails.
- **Appearance:**
  - Selectable faces, hairstyles and body builds, plus colours, scars and markings.
  - Presets with many combinations rather than fine sliders, so gear fits every supported build.
  - No gender restrictions on hairstyles, clothing or accessories.
- **Changing your look:** race is fixed for a character. Hair, facial hair, colours, scars and
  markings can be changed later at an appearance service in settlements.

## Specialties and combat

- **Specialties:** warrior, archer, mage, necromancer, rogue and healer.
  - Each levels separately through use, with trainers and quests unlocking advanced abilities.
  - You can learn several and switch between them.
- **Hybrids:** a main specialty plus a limited selection of trained abilities from another, if
  your equipment supports them.
- **Loadouts:**
  - Complete loadouts (gear plus specialty) change anywhere outside combat, if you carry the gear.
  - Two prepared weapon sets swap during combat, with a short switching animation.
- **Combat:**
  - Direct movement, attacks, aiming, blocking and dodging, with optional target lock and aim
    assistance.
  - Weapon families handle differently: swords are balanced, spears have reach, daggers are quick.
- **Magic gear:** two-handed staves, one-handed wands, and off-hand spellbooks and focuses.
  Necromancers get their own recognisable designs.
- **Necromancy:** controversial in the world, but a necromancer can still be a hero; reputation
  follows your actions.
- **Companions:** pets don't fight; fighting companions come from trained abilities such as
  necromancy.

## Equipment

- **What you see is what you wear.**
  - Base clothing sits under armour, and each armour piece decides which sleeves, collars and
    trousers stay visible.
  - Weapons have proper grips, sheathed positions, and draw and holster animations.
  - Prepared weapons and selected tools stay visible; ordinary inventory stays abstract.
- **Armour:** any sufficiently trained character can wear any armour, trading protection,
  mobility and magical effectiveness.
- **Looks:**
  - Most gear is practical and proportionate; exceptional gear gets bolder silhouettes and
    restrained effects.
  - Dyes and decorative variations colour selected fabric and trim, while named items stay
    recognisable.
- **The back slot:** capes, cloaks and backpacks share one back slot for now. Backpacks add
  inventory space; capes give combat benefits.
- **Inventory:** limited slots, and materials stack.
- **Getting gear:** crafting, shops, quests, enemy drops, bosses and factions all offer good gear.
  - Requirements are skill levels, sometimes plus quests.
  - Named items have fixed identities and deliberate upgrade paths (materials, crafting, quests).
- **Crafting:** smithing, tailoring, leatherworking and enchanting are learned independently.
  Crafted gear stays competitive at high level, partly through rare boss materials.
- **No durability loss.**
- **Death:**
  - You keep your equipment, inventory and skills.
  - You respawn somewhere safe.
  - Some of your coins drop in a recoverable pouch.

## Factions

- **The rivals:** three major powers, each with understandable goals, internal disagreements and
  its own look:
  - protecting nature;
  - building civilisation and trade;
  - recovering ancient magical knowledge.
- **Guilds:** neutral professional guilds sit alongside them. You can join several guilds, but
  must choose between rival factions.
- **Changing sides:**
  - You can switch allegiance, but must earn the new faction's trust.
  - Earned gear stays yours; privileges follow current standing.
- **Open to all:** every race and specialty is open in every faction. Faction colours and
  symbols adapt to each race.

## World, NPCs and creatures

- **Tone:** warm, adventurous and occasionally funny, with genuinely eerie ruins, dangerous
  wilderness and darker necromancy, but no graphic gore.
- **The world:** deliberately designed regions, landmarks, settlements and routes, with shortcuts
  and discoveries.
  - The first region is a temperate valley: woodland, grassland, farms, rivers and old stone
    ruins.
  - Exploring, crafting, trading and training are all worthwhile without following the main
    story.
- **Humanoid NPCs:**
  - They use the players' races, appearance options and equipment.
  - Their outfits come from occupation, region and faction, with distinctive pieces for
    important characters.
  - They visibly do their jobs and move between work and rest.
- **Enemies:** wild beasts, hostile humanoids, undead and ancient constructs, each with several
  distinct creatures, behaviours and regional variants.
- **Bosses:** major bosses get unique designs, animations and attack patterns. Minibosses can be
  enhanced ordinary enemies. Fewer, thoroughly developed major bosses.
- **Hard rule:**
  - No spiders, other arachnids, similar-looking creatures or web-themed scenery, anywhere.
  - That covers enemies, bosses, summons, effects and decorations.

## Scope and presentation

- **The first version** is solo and offline; co-op comes later, with multiplayer in mind
  throughout.
- **The first complete area:** one settlement, surrounding wilderness, one dungeon and one major
  boss.
- **Dialogue:** written conversations, expressive poses and short vocal reactions; full voice
  acting is a later decision.
- **Performance:** a smooth 60 fps during gameplay. Shadows, foliage density and distant detail
  adjust as needed, while nearby characters and gear keep their quality.
- **Assets:** free and paid licensed assets are acceptable starting points, adapted to one visual
  direction. Paid ones are presented with cost and licence before any purchase.
- **Quality first:** before the full catalogue is made, Tariq reviews an interactive character
  workshop. If quality takes longer, the first roster shrinks rather than the standard.
